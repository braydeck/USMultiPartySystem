Four kinds of ANES voters

The four stable segments ANES 2024 forms from its own ten attitude factors (n = 4,673 respondents who answered at least half of each factor's items, post-election weights). "Where they stand" reads the factors the segments were built on; everything else describes them on things the clustering never used. Names are working labels for this page. In the table, violet cells sit clearly above the full sample and orange cells clearly below it, judged by a 1,000-draw bootstrap (P ≥ 0.95); uncolored cells are within the noise. In the priority lists, ▲ and ▼ mark the same test. The two middle segments hold up less well under resampling than the two outer ones.

Secular progressives

Harris voters for whom religion plays little part: the most educated, least rural and most hopeful of the four.

21% of the sample (n = 1,038) · 92% Harris, 0% Trump, 6% did not vote or no data · party ID 98% D, 1% R

Religion is very or extremely important to 17% of this segment, against 46% of the sample, and 53% are atheist, agnostic or nothing in particular. 67% hold a bachelor's degree, 20% identify as gay, lesbian, bisexual or something else, and 94% voted. They rate Donald Trump at 3 out of 100 and Planned Parenthood at 86. US democracy (95%), climate change (93%) and abortion (87%) top their issue list; illegal immigration is rated very important by 30%, against 65% overall. They want more government services (5.7 on a 1 to 7 scale, against 4.6 overall) and favor weapons for Ukraine (1.9 on 1 to 7, 1 = favor a great deal, against 3.5).

Center-left moderates

The largest segment: near the sample average on most factors, left of it on guns, less engaged than the progressives and, with them, the least angry and afraid of the four.

34% of the sample (n = 1,592) · 56% Harris, 20% Trump, 21% did not vote or no data · party ID 62% D, 30% R

56% voted for Harris, 20% for Trump and 21% did not vote or have no vote recorded. 62% identify as Democrats and 30% as Republicans, and they place themselves at 3.8 on the 1 to 7 ideology scale, just left of the midpoint. They sit left of the sample on guns and mildly left on the economy, and within about a tenth of a standard deviation of the sample average on the other eight factors. 56% are white, 18% Black and 15% Hispanic, 58% are women, and religion is very or extremely important to 44%. 44% follow politics most of the time, against 55% overall. 34% feel very or extremely angry about the country and 38% very or extremely afraid. 38% watch the network evening news, the most of the four, and climate change is very important to 69%, against 57% overall. They rate Kamala Harris at 60 and Donald Trump at 26.

Young, disaffected working class

The youngest, poorest and least engaged segment: right of the sample on guns and religion, the lowest on democratic norms and the most opposed to weapons for Ukraine.

17% of the sample (n = 647) · 18% Harris, 27% Trump, 54% did not vote or no data · party ID 38% D, 46% R

54% did not vote or have no vote recorded, 27% voted for Trump and 18% for Harris. 40% are under 35, 58% have no education past high school and 31% have household incomes under $30,000. 38% are white, 26% Hispanic, 23% Black and 8% Asian American. They rate checks and balances at 3.5 on a 1 to 5 importance scale, against 4.3 overall. They oppose weapons for Ukraine (4.7 on 1 to 7, 7 = oppose a great deal, against 3.5) and military aid to Israel (4.5 against 3.8), and want more government services (5.0 against 4.6). Asked whether violence to pursue political goals is justified, they average 2.15 on a 1 to 5 scale (1 = not at all), against 1.33 overall. 24% are very or extremely worried about their finances, the most of the four.

Where they stand

  • rates checks and balances, accountability and a free press as less important (+1.1 SD)
  • opposes military aid to Israel and weapons for Ukraine (+1.0 SD)
  • opposes gun restrictions (+0.8 SD)
  • more religious and socially traditional (+0.5 SD)

Religious conservatives

Older, rural, devout Trump voters, the angriest and most afraid about the country of the four.

28% of the sample (n = 1,396) · 3% Harris, 80% Trump, 15% did not vote or no data · party ID 4% D, 93% R

Religion is very or extremely important to 66% and 48% are born-again Christians. 59% think of themselves as small-town or rural people, 66% are married, 30% are 65 or older and 41% are women. 58% feel very or extremely angry about the country and 59% very or extremely afraid. They rate the police at 82, MAGA at 71 and Planned Parenthood at 36, and 41% watch Fox News shows regularly. Cost of living (97%), schools (93%), crime (93%) and illegal immigration (93%) top their list; climate change is very important to 18%. They favor military aid to Israel (2.6 on 1 to 7, 1 = favor a great deal, against 3.8).

Where they stand
