Secular progressives

Harris voters for whom religion plays little part, the most secular and most urban of the five.

17% of the sample (n = 672) · 87% Harris, 2% Trump, 10% did not vote or no data · party ID 93% D, 5% R

Religion is very or extremely important to 10% of this segment, against 45% of the sample, and 57% are atheist, agnostic or nothing in particular. 59% hold a bachelor's degree and 25% identify as gay, lesbian, bisexual or something else. They rate Donald Trump at 3 out of 100, Christian fundamentalists at 17 and the Supreme Court at 27. US democracy (95%) and climate change (88%) top their issue list; illegal immigration is rated very important by 35%, against 64% overall.

Institution-trusting Democrats

Older, engaged Harris voters who trust the people counting the votes and feel hopeful about the country.

15% of the sample (n = 588) · 90% Harris, 3% Trump, 5% did not vote or no data · party ID 93% D, 5% R

95% voted, and 31% feel very or extremely hopeful about the country, against 17% overall; 20% feel very or extremely angry (42% overall). They trust their local election officials at 4.4 on a 1 to 5 scale, against 3.5 overall. 34% are 65 or older and 61% are women. They watch the network evening news (42%), MSNBC's evening shows (30%) and PBS NewsHour (19%). Religion is very or extremely important to 33%, against 10% of the secular progressives, the segment they border.

Multiracial religious traditionalists

A working-class, religious segment split evenly between Harris and Trump, and the most likely of the five to say they haven't thought much about a policy.

25% of the sample (n = 854) · 34% Harris, 34% Trump, 30% did not vote or no data · party ID 44% D, 47% R

47% of this segment is white, 22% Black and 18% Hispanic, against 65%, 11% and 14% overall. 45% have no education past high school, 44% have children at home, and religion is very or extremely important to 62%. They are cooler than the sample toward gay men and lesbians (48 vs 62), transgender people (42 vs 55) and Jews (61 vs 71). 33% answered "haven't thought much" on at least one policy scale, and 70% voted.

Financially squeezed skeptics

The largest segment: Republican-leaning but loosely attached, under financial strain, distrustful of government and elections, and focused on prices.

27% of the sample (n = 860) · 29% Harris, 40% Trump, 29% did not vote or no data · party ID 37% D, 55% R

66% cut their spending this year and 26% borrowed to pay bills, the highest of the five on both. Asked how often they trust the federal government, they average 3.81 on a 1 to 5 scale (5 = never), against 3.52 overall. 47% follow politics always or most of the time, the lowest of the five, and 71% voted. Cost of living is very important to 94%; abortion (51%), gun policy (53%) and climate change (48%) rank lower here than in the sample. They rate the NRA at 56, Donald Trump at 49 and Kamala Harris at 37.

Religious conservatives

Older, rural, devout Trump voters, the angriest and most afraid about the country of the five.

16% of the sample (n = 640) · 7% Harris, 78% Trump, 14% did not vote or no data · party ID 6% D, 90% R

Religion is very or extremely important to 84% and 65% are born-again Christians. 60% think of themselves as small-town or rural people, 68% are married and 35% are 65 or older. 59% feel very or extremely angry about the country and 60% very or extremely afraid. They rate MAGA at 72 and Planned Parenthood at 28, and place themselves at 5.5 on the 1 to 7 abortion scale (7 = never permitted), against 3.2 overall. 40% watch Fox News shows regularly. Climate change is very important to 19%.
