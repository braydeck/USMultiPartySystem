Four kinds of ANES voters

The four stable segments ANES 2024 forms from its own ten attitude factors (n = 3,132 respondents complete on the clustering items, post-election weights). "Where they stand" reads the factors the segments were built on; everything else describes them on things the clustering never used. Names are working labels for this page. In the table, violet cells sit clearly above the full sample and orange cells clearly below it, judged by a 1,000-draw bootstrap (P ≥ 0.95); uncolored cells are within the noise. In the priority lists, ▲ and ▼ mark the same test. The two middle segments hold up less well under resampling than the two outer ones.

Secular progressives

Harris voters for whom religion plays little part: the most educated, most urban and most hopeful of the four.

27% of the sample (n = 869) · 92% Harris, 1% Trump, 7% did not vote or no data · party ID 97% D, 2% R

Religion is very or extremely important to 16% of this segment, against 44% of the sample, and 53% are atheist, agnostic or nothing in particular. 64% hold a bachelor's degree, 22% identify as gay, lesbian, bisexual or something else, and 93% voted. They rate Donald Trump at 3 out of 100 and Planned Parenthood at 86. US democracy (94%), climate change (92%) and abortion (89%) top their issue list; illegal immigration is rated very important by 30%, against 63% overall. They want more government services (5.8 on a 1 to 7 scale, against 4.6 overall) and favor weapons for Ukraine (2.0 on 1 to 7, 1 = favor a great deal).

Moderate Democrats

Center-left Harris voters, less engaged than the progressives and the calmest of the four about the country.

27% of the sample (n = 829) · 63% Harris, 15% Trump, 18% did not vote or no data · party ID 66% D, 25% R

63% voted for Harris and 15% for Trump, and 66% identify as Democrats, but they place themselves at 3.7 on the 1 to 7 ideology scale, just left of the midpoint. They sit mildly left of the sample on guns, religion, the economy and immigration, and near the sample average on the rest. 58% are white, 15% Black and 15% Hispanic, and religion is very or extremely important to 40%. 52% follow politics most of the time, against 61% overall. 31% feel very or extremely angry about the country and 36% very or extremely afraid, the lowest of the four on both. 40% watch the network evening news, the most of the four. They rate Kamala Harris at 63 and Donald Trump at 22.

Young, disaffected working class

The youngest, poorest and least engaged segment: wants more from government, leans right on immigration and guns, and is the most skeptical of democratic norms and of foreign aid.

16% of the sample (n = 450) · 19% Harris, 34% Trump, 45% did not vote or no data · party ID 37% D, 52% R

45% did not vote or have no vote recorded, 34% voted for Trump and 19% for Harris. 38% are under 35, 48% have no education past high school and 24% have household incomes under $30,000. 45% are white, 23% Hispanic, 17% Black and 10% Asian American. They rate checks and balances at 3.7 on a 1 to 5 importance scale, against 4.5 overall. They oppose military aid to Israel (4.9 on 1 to 7, 7 = oppose a great deal, against 3.8) and weapons for Ukraine (4.8 against 3.3), and want more government services (5.1 against 4.6). Asked whether violence to pursue political goals is justified, they average 2.05 on a 1 to 5 scale (1 = not at all), against 1.28 overall. 24% are very or extremely worried about their finances.

Where they stand

  • opposes aid to Israel and Ukraine (-1.1 SD)
  • rates checks and balances, accountability and a free press as less important (+1.0 SD)
  • opposes gun restrictions (+0.6 SD)
  • feels shut out of government; agrees more that women seek power over men (+0.6 SD)

Religious conservatives

Older, rural, devout Trump voters, the angriest and most afraid about the country of the four.

30% of the sample (n = 984) · 5% Harris, 79% Trump, 15% did not vote or no data · party ID 6% D, 92% R

Religion is very or extremely important to 69% and 52% are born-again Christians. 58% think of themselves as small-town or rural people, 65% are married, 30% are 65 or older and 35% are women. 56% feel very or extremely angry about the country and 59% very or extremely afraid. They rate the police at 82, MAGA at 71 and Planned Parenthood at 33, and 42% watch Fox News shows regularly. Cost of living (97%), schools (93%), crime (92%) and illegal immigration (91%) top their list; climate change is very important to 17%. They favor military aid to Israel (2.4 on 1 to 7, 1 = favor a great deal).
