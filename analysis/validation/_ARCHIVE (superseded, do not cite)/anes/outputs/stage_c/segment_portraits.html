Four kinds of ANES voters

The four stable segments ANES 2024 forms from eight of its own attitude factors (n = 4,634 respondents who answered at least half of each of those factors' items, post-election weights). Two more factors, trust in institutions and leaving decisions to business leaders or experts, did not qualify to define segments because each comes from a single question battery; they appear below as descriptions. "Where they stand" reads the factors the segments were built on; everything else describes them on things the clustering never used. Names are working labels for this page. In the table, violet cells sit clearly above the full sample and orange cells clearly below it, judged by a 1,000-draw bootstrap (P ≥ 0.95); uncolored cells are within the noise. In the priority lists, ▲ and ▼ mark the same test. Three of the four segments hold up under resampling (mean overlap of 0.75 or more across 30 resamples); the fourth sits between stable and dissolved.

Secular progressives

Harris voters for whom religion plays little part: the most educated, least rural and most hopeful of the four.

18% of the sample (n = 909) · 92% Harris, 1% Trump, 6% did not vote or no data · party ID 98% D, 2% R

Religion is very or extremely important to 15% of this segment, against 45% of the sample, and 53% are atheist, agnostic or nothing in particular. 67% hold a bachelor's degree, 22% identify as gay, lesbian, bisexual or something else, and 94% voted. They rate Donald Trump at 2 out of 100 and Planned Parenthood at 87. US democracy (97%), climate change (93%) and abortion (87%) top their issue list; illegal immigration is rated very important by 30%, against 65% overall. They want more government services (5.7 on a 1 to 7 scale, against 4.7 overall) and favor weapons for Ukraine (1.7 on 1 to 7, 1 = favor a great deal, against 3.5). Of the four, they are the most opposed to leaving political decisions to business leaders, experts or a strong leader.

Center-left moderates

Harris-leaning voters left of the sample on every clustering factor but foreign threats, and the least angry and afraid about the country of the four.

26% of the sample (n = 1,178) · 71% Harris, 10% Trump, 15% did not vote or no data · party ID 78% D, 17% R

71% voted for Harris, 10% for Trump and 15% did not vote or have no vote recorded. 78% identify as Democrats and 17% as Republicans, and they place themselves at 3.5 on the 1 to 7 ideology scale. They support gun restrictions about as strongly as the secular progressives and sit milder than the progressives on immigration, religion, the economy and democratic norms. 56% are white, 17% Black and 15% Hispanic, 55% are women, and religion is very or extremely important to 43%. 28% feel very or extremely angry about the country and 34% very or extremely afraid. 40% watch the network evening news, the most of the four, and climate change is very important to 78%, against 57% overall. They favor weapons for Ukraine (2.9 on 1 to 7, 1 = favor a great deal, against 3.5). They rate Kamala Harris at 69 and Donald Trump at 16.

Young, disaffected working class

The youngest, poorest and least engaged segment: wants a larger government role in the economy, sits right of the sample on guns, rates democratic norms lowest and opposes weapons for Ukraine most.

26% of the sample (n = 1,043) · 22% Harris, 30% Trump, 46% did not vote or no data · party ID 39% D, 47% R

46% did not vote or have no vote recorded, 30% voted for Trump and 22% for Harris. 35% are under 35, 53% have no education past high school, 26% have household incomes under $30,000 and 28% borrowed money to pay bills this year. 47% are white, 21% Black, 21% Hispanic and 6% Asian American, and 45% have children under 18 at home. They rate checks and balances at 3.6 on a 1 to 5 importance scale, against 4.3 overall. They oppose weapons for Ukraine (4.7 on 1 to 7, 7 = oppose a great deal, against 3.5) and want more government services (5.3 against 4.7). Asked whether violence to pursue political goals is justified, they average 1.69 on a 1 to 5 scale (1 = not at all), against 1.32 overall. 22% are very or extremely worried about their finances, the most of the four.

Where they stand

  • rates checks and balances, accountability and a free press as less important (+1.0 SD)
  • opposes aid to Ukraine and Israel; would rather the US stayed home (+0.6 SD)
  • opposes gun restrictions (+0.5 SD)
  • wants a larger government role in the economy (-0.4 SD)

Religious conservatives

Older, rural, devout Trump voters, the angriest and most afraid about the country of the four.

30% of the sample (n = 1,504) · 6% Harris, 77% Trump, 16% did not vote or no data · party ID 5% D, 91% R

Religion is very or extremely important to 64% and 47% are born-again Christians. 58% think of themselves as small-town or rural people, 64% are married, 31% are 65 or older and 42% are women. 57% feel very or extremely angry about the country and 57% very or extremely afraid. They rate the police at 81, rural Americans at 79, MAGA at 68 and Planned Parenthood at 36, and 42% watch Fox News shows regularly. Cost of living (96%), crime (92%), schools (91%) and illegal immigration (90%) top their list; climate change is very important to 20%. They favor military aid to Israel (2.7 on 1 to 7, 1 = favor a great deal, against 3.8).

Where they stand
