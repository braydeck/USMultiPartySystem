# Party descriptions: the nine

Draft 2026-11-04 for the researcher to edit. These replace `PARTY_TAGLINES` and the paragraph descriptions in
`viz/src/constants/parties.ts` when the rebuild lands. Each party has a **Name**, a **Tagline** (one line) and a
**Paragraph**. The figures under each are the evidence behind the wording, from
`analysis/efa/exploration/listwise_check/outputs/compare_typologies_k9/index.html` (CES 2024, weighted). They are
not app copy.

---

## CON (cluster A, 17.6%)

**Name:** Conservative

**Tagline:** Pro-order and pro-border, low-tax, and backs Medicaid expansion

**Paragraph:** The mainstream right. Backs the border wall, more police funding, more fossil-fuel production and
extending the 2017 tax cuts. Supports Medicaid expansion and splits on repealing the Affordable Care Act. Older,
mostly white homeowners.

> Figures: wall 93%, more police 84%, fossil fuels 90%, tax cuts 81%, Medicaid 70%, ACA repeal 56%. Median age 62,
> white 82%, own home 78%.

## NAT (cluster B, 18.0%)

**Name:** Nationalist

**Tagline:** Anti-immigration, culturally traditional, low-tax, and distrusts government

**Paragraph:** The hard right. Backs the border wall, more fossil-fuel production, repealing the Affordable Care Act
and parental consent before schools use a new name or pronoun. Opposes higher taxes on top earners. The most
culturally conservative party, and distrusts the federal government. Older, white, married and Protestant.

> Figures: wall 99%, fossil fuels 99%, ACA repeal 91%, parental consent 99%, taxes on $400k+ 13%; cultural
> conservatism +1.07; little or no trust in the federal government 85%. Married 69%, Protestant 54%.

## POP (cluster C, 2.9%)

**Name:** Populist

**Tagline:** Distrusts elections and government; opposes more public spending

**Paragraph:** The anti-government party. The most distrustful of elections and the federal government, and the
most opposed to new spending on schools, health care, roads and police. Splits on the border wall and abortion.
Younger, mostly independent, and often religiously unaffiliated.

> Figures: elections fair 28%, little or no trust 88%, economic right +1.38; wall 53%, always allow abortion 59%.
> Median age 46, independent 47%, religion "nothing in particular" 32%.

## STY (cluster D, 22.1%)

**Name:** Solidarity

**Tagline:** Backs student-debt relief and Medicaid, along with tax cuts and school vouchers

**Paragraph:** The cross-pressured working class, and the largest party. Backs student-debt relief, Medicaid
expansion and a path to legal status, along with extending the 2017 tax cuts, school vouchers and parental consent
on pronouns. Younger, racially diverse and lower-income; members come from both major parties.

> Figures: student debt 85%, Medicaid 92%, legal status 73%, tax cuts 75%, vouchers 73%, parental consent 71%.
> Median age 48, Black 24%, family income under $50k 56% (highest). Democrat 40%, Republican 26%.

## LBR (cluster E, 11.7%)

**Name:** Labor

**Tagline:** Progressive on culture, climate and civil liberties; shift police funding

**Paragraph:** The civil-libertarian left. Backs abortion rights, a path to legal status, climate rules and higher
taxes on top earners, and a majority would move police funding to other services. College-educated, secular and
suburban.

> Figures: always allow abortion 96%, legal status 89%, renewable standard 96%, taxes on $400k+ 92%, move 10% of
> police funding 56%. Bachelor's 46%, atheist or agnostic 32%, suburb 46%.

## CUP (cluster F, 10.8%)

**Name:** Civic Union

**Tagline:** Socially liberal, centrist on economics, pro-police, and trusts institutions

**Paragraph:** The suburban moderate. Backs abortion rights, an assault-weapons ban and Medicaid expansion; splits on
the 2017 tax cuts and mostly opposes student-debt relief. Supports more police funding and trusts elections and the
federal government. Older homeowners, with the highest share of self-described moderates.

> Figures: always allow abortion 82%, assault-weapons ban 79%, Medicaid 81%, tax cuts 42%, student debt 37%, more
> police 62%, elections fair 82%. Moderate 56% (highest), own home 73%.

## OAO (cluster G, 7.5%)

**Name:** Order and Opportunity

**Tagline:** Left on economics, moderate on culture, pro-police, and trusts government

**Paragraph:** The working-class left of order. Backs Medicaid expansion, student-debt relief, higher taxes on top
earners and more police funding; supports school vouchers and parental consent on pronouns. The most trusting of
the federal government. Older, urban, lower-income, and about a third Black.

> Figures: Medicaid 99%, student debt 95%, taxes on $400k+ 90%, more police 68%, vouchers 61%, parental consent 75%;
> little or no trust 35% (lowest). Black 31%, city 39%, family income under $50k 54%.

## PRG (cluster H, 6.1%)

**Name:** Progressive

**Tagline:** Progressive across the board; backs arming Ukraine and trusts elections

**Paragraph:** The college-educated progressive left. Backs abortion rights, a path to legal status, climate rules
and an assault-weapons ban, and a majority would move police funding to other services. The strongest support for
sending arms to Ukraine. Trusts elections. The most college-educated party.

> Figures: always allow abortion 98%, legal status 95%, assault-weapons ban 97%, move police funding 59%, arms to
> Ukraine 82% (highest), elections fair 82%, bachelor's 59% (highest).

## DSA (cluster I, 3.3%)

**Name:** Democratic Socialists

**Tagline:** Socialist left: shift police funding, secular, and distrusts government

**Paragraph:** The socialist left. Near-unanimous for moving police funding to other services, abortion rights and a
path to legal status. Distrusts the federal government. The youngest party, with the highest shares of LGBT and
atheist or agnostic members.

> Figures: move police funding 100%, always allow abortion 100%, legal status 99%; little or no trust 86%. Median age
> 37 (youngest), LGBT 46%, atheist or agnostic 50% (both highest).
