# Essay sources & phrasing (verified 2026-07-14)

Citations and paste-ready phrasings for the FPTP/polarization essay. Numbers verified against the
module data and the cited papers; items flagged **VERIFY** still need a manual check.

## Citations

- **Nemoto, K., & Franco de Campos Pinto, P. (2018).** *Civility and Hostility in Parliamentary
  Politics.* Working paper (presented at the Asian Political Methodology Meeting, 2019; Musashi
  University). — cite as a working/conference paper (not peer-reviewed). GloVe embeddings + Emolex
  anger lexicon on all 821,442 NZ Hansard speeches 1987–2016.
- **Boxell, L., Gentzkow, M., & Shapiro, J. M. (2024).** Cross-Country Trends in Affective
  Polarization. *Review of Economics and Statistics, 106*(2), 557–565. (NBER Working Paper 26669.)
- **Mason, L., & Warren, S. — SNF Agora Institute, Johns Hopkins University, with YouGov (2024).**
  September 2024 poll, ~2,000 eligible voters.
  <https://hub.jhu.edu/2024/10/27/snf-agora-poll-september-2024/>
- **Bernaerts, K., Blanckaert, B., & Caluwaerts, D. (2023).** Institutional design and polarization.
  Do consensus democracies fare better in fighting polarization than majoritarian democracies?
  *Democratization, 30*(2), 153–172.
- **Hudde, A., Horne, W., Adams, J., & Gidron, N. (2024).** How warm are political interactions? A new
  measure of affective fractionalization. *PLOS ONE, 19*(5), e0294401.
- **V-Dem** — Coppedge et al., *Varieties of Democracy* dataset, variable `v2cacamps` ("political
  polarization"). Scores quoted are the 2025 estimates. (Source CSV = OWID republication of V-Dem.)

## Paste-ready phrasings

- **Ireland:** "Ireland has the lowest polarization score of any democracy in the world (−2.49) —
  4.79 points below the United States, and second-lowest of any country (behind only Oman, an
  autocracy)." *(Stronger than "second least-polarized nation," which is ambiguous.)*
- **US company it keeps:** "The United States sits at +2.30 — closer to Afghanistan (+2.32), South
  Sudan (+2.23), and Venezuela (+2.23) than to any established democracy." *(Accurate.)*
- **US vs peers:** keep "dramatically higher than its peer English-speaking countries" (next-highest
  anglophone peer, the UK, is −0.28). Do NOT say "most polarized in the world" — the US is 16th
  globally.
- **Boxell et al.:** "Six of twelve wealthy democracies became *less* polarized over the period —
  including the proportional systems of Germany and Sweden — while the United States saw by far the
  largest increase." *(The paper analyzes 12 countries, not 9; Germany/Sweden fell.)*
  **VERIFY the exact "27.4 → 56.3" figures and the 1978–2020 endpoints against the paper.**
- **New Zealand (Nemoto):** the paragraph as drafted is faithful — keep the "suggestive evidence"
  hedge; it matches the authors' own language. Structural break located near Q2 1996; cohort analysis
  shows incumbent MPs moderated (institutional, not compositional).
- **Hudde mechanism (tighten):** replace "high mathematical probability of regular cross-party
  interaction" with: "In a two-party system your single hostile out-group is roughly half the country;
  in a multiparty system it is a ~10% fringe. So the same out-group contempt translates into far more
  day-to-day hostile contact in the US." *(A balanced two-party split has a lower total cross-party
  rate than a fragmented one; the real point is exposure to your one maximal opponent, which the US
  maximizes: top-2 vote share ≈ 0.99.)*

## What the essay should NOT lean on

- The module's own CSES coldness headline as a hard number (+0.56/97%). After a common-law control
  and a clean single-member-plurality FPTP definition it is +0.30–0.44, ~85% posterior — directional,
  not decisive. Lean on V-Dem (expert) + Boxell + the case contrasts instead.
- "FPTP causes polarization" as a clean causal claim (few FPTP cases; confounded with anglophone
  common-law heritage; the US is essentially the only FPTP+presidential established democracy).
- The party-*count* (effective number of parties) channel — it does not mediate; the balanced-duopoly
  *concentration* channel does, weakly, and mostly for the US.
