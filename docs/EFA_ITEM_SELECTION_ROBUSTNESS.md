# Item Selection & Robustness

A full account of how the ~24 survey items behind the political typology were chosen, why the choices are defensible rather than post-hoc, and what happened when the typology was stress-tested against every plausible alternative. Companion to [`EFA_FACTORS.md`](EFA_FACTORS.md) (factor definitions) and [`METHODOLOGY.md`](METHODOLOGY.md).

**One-line summary:** the production **5-factor / 10-party** model survives every adversarial test — a mechanical item-selection rule, four overlooked policy domains, alternative factor counts, and alternative rotations. No tested change improves it. The audit also produced two substantive findings worth stating on their own: **the culture war and climate are both collinear with left–right in this electorate — not cross-cutting axes**; the genuinely cross-cutting cleavages are the **trust dimensions**.

---

## 1. The selection funnel

Items were reduced in two stages:

```
84 CES policy/attitude battery items      (the full eligible universe)
   │  categorical eligibility rule (Stage A)
   ▼
55 items on the screening sheet           (efa_variable_list.csv)
   │  uniform statistical screen (Stage B): missingness, variance,
   │  partisan collinearity, polychoric redundancy
   ▼
25 items pass into the EFA
   │  one near-Heywood item dropped (CC24_340a, λ≈−0.95)
   ▼
24 items — the production set
```

The 84→55 step (Stage A) was previously undocumented; it is specified below. Stage B is recorded item-by-item in `analysis/efa/efa_variable_list.csv` (final decisions) and `_v3.csv` (the recommendation pass with `redundant_with` annotations).

---

## 2. Stage A — categorical eligibility rule

**Eligible = policy/attitude items measured on a common ordinal or binary scale.** Excluded *by category* (a design decision, not a statistical one):

| Excluded category | Examples | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Evaluations / approvals | `CC24_312*` (Biden/Harris/SCOTUS/Congress approval) | Measure *approval of actors*, not policy attitudes; also near-pure partisan proxies (party-ID R² 0.57–0.63). |
| Ideology ratings / self-placement | `CC24_330*` (rate Biden/Trump/parties; self-placement) | Partisan proxies (self-placement ideology R²=0.82). |
| Retrospective economic sentiment | `CC24_301` (national economy better/worse) | A Biden-approval proxy in 2024 (party-ID R²=0.34). |
| Multi-select / compositional | `CC24_309d*` (emergency-expense options), `CC24_420*` (military-force checklist), `CC24_308*` (Ukraine/Gaza) | Mutually-exclusive checkboxes create structurally invalid polychoric correlations. |
| Non-attitudinal | media use, vote choice, participation, political knowledge, party-ID strength | Not policy attitudes. |

This rule cleanly removes the "obvious" cases on principle: a reader can disagree with the *rule*, but not accuse the analysis of cherry-picking individual items.

---

## 3. Stage B — uniform statistical screen

Applied to every eligible item, all at the **factor layer** (no clustering required):

1. **Missingness** — drop if > ~30% missing.
2. **Variance / valence** — drop ceiling/floor items (modal-category share > ~85%): e.g. background-checks (93% support), abortion-illegal-always (89% oppose).
3. **Partisan collinearity** — flag items with high party-ID R²; drop pure partisan proxies (handled largely by Stage A).
4. **Redundancy** — for any pair with weighted **polychoric** |r| > 0.70, drop the higher-party-ID-R² member (keep the less-partisan twin).
5. **Loading / communality** — after extraction, drop items that fail to load (max |λ| < 0.30 or communality h² < 0.20).

### 3a. Redundancy screening was recomputed (a bug fix)

The committed `redundant_pairs.csv` had been generated by an **earlier, buggy** polychoric pass; its stored correlations were inflated. Re-running the repo's own corrected weighted-polychoric MLE gives materially different numbers:

| pair | buggy CSV | corrected polychoric |
|---|---|---|
| CC24_341a × 341b | 0.743 | **0.538** |
| CC24_440a × 440b | 0.71 | **0.658** |
| CC24_325 × 324d | 0.764 | 0.711 |

**Crucially, no drop *decision* was wrong — only the documented justifications.** Re-derived on correct numbers, every dropped item stays out for a defensible reason:

| dropped item | corrected basis |
|---|---|
| CC24_341b (corporate tax) | redundant with the *retained* CC24_341c at 0.80 (not 341a) |
| CC24_440a (white advantages) | most partisan racial item (party-ID R²=0.37); 0.66 with 440b |
| CC24_441a / 441b (racial resentment) | thin the racial-resentment cluster (0.77 with each other; retained anchors 440b/440c) |
| pew_bornagain, religimp, prayer | redundant with retained pew_churatd (0.61–0.80) |

Regenerated artifacts: `analysis/efa/redundant_pairs.csv`, `redundancy_recheck_notes.md`, `recompute_redundant_pairs.py`.

**Retained multiple-indicator pairs.** Four *kept* pairs exceed the 0.70 rule and were retained deliberately as multiple indicators / factor anchors: 323a~323d (0.81, immigration), 321d~321e (0.76, policing), 421_1~421_2 (0.74, elections), 323b~340f (0.70, enforcement). These are documented rather than pruned — multiple indicators stabilize a factor.

### 3b. Weak retained items: CC24_302 / CC24_303

Two economic-*perception* items (household-income change, price change) are retained despite loading below the 0.30 bar (λ=0.17/0.22, h²=0.15/0.24) — they are the only sub-threshold items in the set. A confirmation re-fit dropping them changes the factor structure negligibly (loadings ±0.02, Φ within 0.01, the F2/F3 trust split intact, CUP if anything sharper). So they are **weak but harmless**: their inclusion is a discretionary domain-coverage choice, their removal is structurally null. They are documented here rather than removed, to keep the published typology stable. (Removing them tightens F2 to a thin 2-item factor — `CC24_421_2` rises to λ≈0.95 — a minor cost.)

## 4. Rotation: oblimin (not Promax)

The pipeline uses **oblimin** oblique rotation (`efa_update.py`, `Rotator(method="oblimin")`), which lets the factors correlate (they do: F1↔F4 = +0.55). Earlier docs said "Promax"; that was an error, now corrected. The distinction matters: on this correlated, cross-loading structure, Promax (a post-hoc power-transform of a varimax solution) is unstable — in testing it spuriously **merged the two trust factors** and caused an anchor collision, while oblimin reproduces the production factor scores cleanly (r≈0.93–0.96). Any replication must use oblimin.

---

## 5. Dimensionality: why k = 5

Parallel analysis (Horn's method, polychoric eigenvalues vs the 95th percentile of simulated data) on the production 24 is **borderline**: factor 5's eigenvalue (1.0258) falls just below the retention threshold (1.0278) — it fails by 0.002. k=5 is therefore retained on **interpretability grounds**, not because the data force it: the 5th factor is what lets the clustering separate CUP (the institutionalist), and the alternative (k=4) merges the two trust dimensions and produces a *worse* junk factor (see [`EFA_FACTORS.md`](EFA_FACTORS.md) → Robustness). This is a disclosed researcher choice, not a data-driven inevitability.

A related finding lives in [`EFA_FACTORS.md`](EFA_FACTORS.md): the fifth factor, "Government Distrust" (F3), is a **near-null residual** (η²=0.057) whose party scores run *opposite* to actual government distrust (party-level r=−0.38); the genuine institutional-distrust signal is carried by F2 (Electoral Skepticism, r=+0.84 with raw distrust). F3 is retained because splitting the trust variance is what isolates CUP, but it is not interpreted as a substantive party-ranking axis.

## 6. Is the selection principled or cherry-picked? — the uniform-rule test

To test whether item selection was post-hoc, a **single pre-specified rule** (Stage B, above) was applied mechanically to the full ~63-item eligible pool, letting the rule — not judgment — decide.

**Result:** the rule did **not** reproduce the production 24 (only 11 overlap). But this does not indict the selection, for three reasons:

1. **The core is rule-defensible.** Eleven items survive both the mechanical rule and production — the guns/immigration/trust/racial-attitude/tax spine (321b, 321e, 323b, 340c, 340f, 341a, 421_2, 423, 424, 440b, 440c). An impersonal rule independently keeps the heart of the typology.
2. **The rule's alternative set is numerically degenerate** — it produced Heywood cases, a non-positive-definite matrix, and eigenvalues suggesting k≈2–3. Feeding all 63 items to PAF yields an unstable solution; this *demonstrates* why some curation is legitimate.
3. **The divergence is a pool-composition artifact.** Production items "fail" the rule mostly because a tightly-correlated state-culture battery (CC24_444) dominates the enlarged pool and steals their variance. In the curated 24 they load cleanly.

**Verdict:** the core is principled; the discretionary retentions are *domain-coverage* choices (tax, immigration, religion), not items tuned to manufacture favored parties. The discretion is real but coverage-driven and now documented.

## 7. Robustness to specification (k and residualization)

The full pipeline was re-run under **k=4/k=5 × residualized/non-residualized** variants and each variant's clusters matched back to the production parties. Three tiers emerge:

| tier | parties | depends on |
|---|---|---|
| **Bulletproof** (survive every variant) | STY, POP, CON, NAT, LBR | nothing |
| **Needs k=5** | CUP | the 5th factor (dissolves into CON at k=4) |
| **Needs residualization** | PRG, DSA, LIB, OAO | F4/F5 residualized on F1 |

So five parties are specification-proof; five each hinge on exactly one documented modeling choice. This is stated honestly rather than hidden: the robust core carries the strong claims; CUP and the left-of-center parties are more model-dependent.

## 8. Overlooked-domain inclusion tests

The uniform-rule test surfaced policy domains never in the original candidate frame. Each was added to the curated set and re-fit (oblimin, k=5 and k=6, then DPGMM) to ask: does it add a coherent *new* dimension while preserving the 10 parties?

| domain added | new dimension? | party effect | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Economic (repeal-ACA, work-req, student-loans, corporate-tax, TikTok) | No — items reinforce the existing spine/enforcement factors | churn, softer parties | **reinforce → don't adopt** |
| Environment / climate (`CC24_326a–f`) | No — all six load on the left–right spine (λ up to 0.87); parallel analysis rejects a 6th factor | churn (CON −0.23, POP −0.23); confidence 0.89→0.77 | **redundant → document-and-exclude** |
| Culture war (`CC24_444a/b/d/e/f`, `445a`) | Numerically yes (parallel analysis → k=6) — but the items fuse into the values spine; the "6th factor" is actually **immigration-status**, not culture | CON *shatters* (no home); confidence 0.89→0.76 | **a k=6 variant, not a patch** |

### 8a. Climate is a left–right gradient (exhibit)

Mean pro-climate support by party, clusters ordered left→right. The correlation between a party's left–right position and its climate support is **−0.994**; no party is off the diagonal.

| party (L→R) | left–right z | mean climate support |
|---|---|---|
| Progressive left (DSA/PRG) | −1.13 | 94% |
| Progressive-liberal (PRG/LIB) | −1.09 | 90% |
| Mainstream liberal (LBR/LIB) | −1.03 | 87% |
| Econ-left / socially-mixed (LIB/OAO) | −0.89 | 76% |
| Center-left independents (STY/LBR) | −0.66 | 71% |
| Populist-immigration center (CON/CUP) | −0.52 | 65% |
| Disengaged center-right (STY/POP) | +0.41 | 42% |
| Mainstream conservative (CON/CUP) | +0.58 | 25% |
| Hard conservative (CON/NAT) | +1.41 | 3% |
| Nationalist right (NAT/CON) | +1.54 | 2% |

We specifically searched for the two signatures that would make climate cross-cutting — an economically-left-but-anti-climate bloc, or a right-but-pro-conservation bloc — and neither exists (largest residual off the linear fit: 6pp). The only texture is a slight center compression on the "gas-stove ban" item (a symbolic culture-war wobble), which is a within-axis ripple, not a separate dimension.

### 8b. The culture-war k=6 solution profiled

Adding the culture battery does push parallel analysis to k=6, but profiling the actual parties shows why it is a *variant*, not an upgrade:
- The six culture items **do not form their own factor** — the "new right" items (trans, pronouns, vouchers, race-in-admissions) fuse into an expanded left–right values factor; abortion-travel joins religious traditionalism; and the sixth factor k=6 buys is **immigration legalization** (dreamers/legal-status), not culture.
- **Conservative (the largest party, ~19%) shatters** across four clusters with no home; POP dissolves; mean assignment confidence falls 0.89→0.76.
- The only genuinely new distinction is a split of the secular left into anti-establishment vs institutionalist blocs — driven by **government distrust, not culture**.

No socially-liberal/economically-conservative bloc and no trad-populist bloc — the groups that would have justified the move — appear. Kept as a documented "k=6 culture-war variant," not adopted.

## 9. Clustering reproducibility caveat

The DPGMM cluster *assignments* are seed-sensitive: re-clustering the **identical** factor scores at different seeds gives ARI 0.55–0.99 (median ~0.65), and a from-scratch re-derivation matches the saved production cluster file at only ARI≈0.24. Therefore:

- The party **structure** — the factors, centroids, and profiles — is robust and reproducible.
- The exact respondent-level **membership** is one draw from a mixture model.

We defend the parties as **stable ideological types**, not as a fixed roster of who belongs to which. (This also means the absolute ARIs in the inclusion tests above are noisy within-harness figures — the reliable signals are parallel analysis, loadings, and assignment confidence, which are consistent across runs.)

---

## 10. Two substantive findings

Independent of any modeling decision, the domain tests establish two claims about the 2024 electorate:

1. **The "new" cleavages pundits treat as separate axes — the culture war and climate — are collinear with left–right.** Climate is a −0.994 gradient down the left–right axis; the culture-war items fuse into the same values spine. Neither pulls cross-pressured voters off the main axis.
2. **The genuinely cross-cutting cleavages are the trust dimensions** — Electoral Skepticism (F2) and Government Distrust (F3). This is *why* the interesting, non-obvious parties (STY, OAO, CUP) exist: they are defined by where they sit on institutional trust, not by being extreme points on the left–right line.

## 11. Summary of every test

| test | question | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Redundancy recompute | were the r>0.70 drops correct? | numbers were buggy; decisions all hold on corrected polychoric |
| Rotation check | Promax or oblimin? | oblimin (doc was wrong; fixed) |
| Weak-item check | do all retained items load? | all but 302/303 (≥0.30); those are weak-but-harmless |
| Parallel analysis | how many factors? | k=5 borderline; retained for CUP; F3 a near-null residual |
| Uniform-rule test | is selection cherry-picked? | core is rule-defensible; discretion is coverage-driven |
| k4/k5 × resid | which parties are robust? | 5 bulletproof; CUP needs k5; PRG/DSA/LIB/OAO need resid |
| Economic add | new dimension? | no — reinforces spine → don't adopt |
| Environment add | new dimension? | no — climate = left–right (r=−0.994) → exclude |
| Culture-war add | new dimension? | k=6 numerically, but fuses to values + shatters CON → variant only |
| DPGMM reseed | how reproducible? | structure robust; membership ~0.65 reproducible (disclose) |

## 12. Honest limitations (for disclosure)

- Item selection involves researcher discretion; it is documented (Stages A/B) and thresholds are stated, but they are choices — a reader may prefer different ones.
- k=5 is a borderline parallel-analysis call retained for interpretability.
- F3 ("Government Distrust") is a near-null residual, not a substantive axis.
- Without 302/303, F2 becomes a thin 2-item factor.
- DPGMM membership is a mixture draw (~0.65 reproducible); the structure, not the roster, is the robust claim.
- Reproductions here use pairwise-complete polychoric matrices, which are non-positive-definite (inherent to the method).

Reproduction scripts and logs: `analysis/efa/` (committed screening + redundancy artifacts) and the experiment scratch under `/tmp/efa_experiment/` (transient).
